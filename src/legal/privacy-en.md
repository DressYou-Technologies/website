## 1. Who is responsible

DressYou is operated by DressYou Technologies, a simplified joint-stock company (SAS) registered with the Paris trade and companies register (RCS) under number 108 706 037, whose registered office is at 47 rue Vivienne, 75002 Paris, France, the controller of the personal data this notice describes. Questions about your data and requests to exercise your rights go to support@dressyouapp.com. DressYou has not appointed a data protection officer.

This notice covers the DressYou apps for iOS and Android, the DressYou browser extension, the website dressyouapp.com and the pages a friend opens from an ask link.

## 2. What DressYou processes, and why

Each purpose below names the data and the legal basis DressYou relies on; section 6 says how long each is kept.

### 2.1 Your account

Your email address; your password, stored only as a hash; your Sign in with Apple identifier, if you use it; your passkeys and two-step verification settings; the version of the terms and of this notice you accepted, and when; an optional profile photo; and, for each signed-in device, the session's dates, IP address and device description. A browser you link through the extension keeps its browser name, platform and dates, and no IP address.

DressYou uses this data to create your account, sign you in, keep the account secure and send the emails it needs: address verification and password reset. Legal basis: the contract (Article 6(1)(b) GDPR), and DressYou's legitimate interest in protecting accounts and detecting abuse (Article 6(1)(f)).

### 2.2 Body capture and your twin

The photographs of you that you provide, taken in the app or imported: the first front, the second back, and any further ones as appearance context; your twin, the two images, front and back, generated from them; and, if you use Adjust, the words you add to describe a change. Before the first upload you confirm that you are 18 or over and that the photographs show only you; DressYou records both confirmations with the notice version and the date.

Your photograph makes your twin and your outfit images, and nothing else: DressYou takes no measurement from a photograph. Your photographs are never sold and never used for ads. Your outfit images show garments on you when you ask, made from your photograph, which is kept until you replace or delete it.

Legal basis: your explicit consent (Article 9(2)(a) GDPR), which you withdraw by removing your capture in the app. DressYou then erases the photographs and the twin, while the outfit images already made stay until you delete them, erase all your data in the settings or delete your account; storage keeps an earlier copy of each for recovery until it is deleted, as section 6 describes. Consenting to body capture is not consenting to the generation or publication of a portrait for a card.

DressYou checks that you're 18 or older before you use the app. On an iPhone with iOS 26 or later, DressYou asks Apple whether you're 18 or older, and refuses anyone under 18. On an older iPhone, or if you decline Apple's prompt, your own declaration that you're 18 or older stands.

<h3 id="ai">2.3 AI: OpenAI and TypeSafe</h3>

DressYou asks your permission once, before any of your photographs or messages reaches an AI provider, a photograph you post aside (below): when you make your twin, add a photographed garment or write to the stylist, whichever comes first. With it, OpenAI receives your front and back photographs, to make your twin, then your photograph for each outfit image, and photographs of your garments, to make their images. It also receives your messages to the stylist and the wardrobe facts each reply needs, to answer you (section 2.6). TypeSafe receives your messages to the stylist and the stylist's replies, to check them. The weather and country code a message carries are described in section 2.12. Once posting opens, a photograph you post is screened by OpenAI's moderation and by TypeSafe whether or not you gave this permission, since screening is a condition of posting (section 2.8). Once its key is set, TypeSafe also reads photographs of your garments, any face cut out on your phone, for their category and the role of each photo.

DressYou keeps your twin photographs until you replace or delete them. They are never measured or used for anything else. DressYou stores your data in the EU (Sweden), and OpenAI processes what it receives in the United States, outside the European Economic Area, until European processing is approved (section 5). You withdraw this permission in privacy & safety, in the app, where deleting your twin photographs is a separate control. Withdrawing refuses every new send at once, other than the screening of a photograph you post (section 2.8), and keeps your twin, photographs and images until you delete them.

DressYou generates images with OpenAI's image model. OpenAI receives, for your twin, all your capture photographs, front, back and any further ones, and any Adjust words; for an outfit image, your photograph and the garment images; for a garment image, the framed garment alone. For an outfit image it also receives a pose description chosen from your pose answer, never the answer itself.

DressYou's production service uses OpenAI's global processing, outside the European Economic Area, until OpenAI approves European processing for DressYou. OpenAI processes this data under its API terms: data sent to its API is not used to train its models, and its abuse-monitoring logs are kept for up to 30 days, unless the law requires longer or longer is reasonably necessary to protect its services or anyone from harm. Deleting data in DressYou does not delete OpenAI's copies before then.

Generated images carry no visible mark, in the app or on a friend's ask page. Planned: signed provenance metadata (C2PA Content Credentials) and an imperceptible watermark on each.

### 2.4 Measurements you enter

Measurements are optional, and none is asked at sign-up or during capture: you add them in the app whenever you choose. They are your height and the tape measurements you enter, and your fit reports on garments you own, including a correction you make to an image of a garment you own; the size of a garment you own also serves as a size anchor. DressYou stores them as ranges, never asks your weight, and takes none from a photograph.

DressYou uses them to tell you which size of a garment will fit and, in the feed, to show you lower a post whose garment comes in no size that fits you (section 2.7). When it has too little to go on, the size answer says so and offers two ways to get one: add a garment you own with its size, or add your measurements; the try-on works either way. Legal basis: your explicit consent, as for body capture; the questions in section 2.2 apply. You can withdraw a measurement at any time; it stops influencing results at once, and its history stays until you delete your account.

### 2.5 The pose answer

Before the scan DressYou asks once, optionally: woman, man or skip. The answer is used only to choose the poses of your outfit images. It is never inferred, and never used for sizing, the stylist, recaps or the product's wording. It is kept with your capture photographs and erased with them, and it never reaches analytics or error reports.

### 2.6 Wardrobe, try-ons and the stylist

The garments you add, from product pages you capture or photographs you take; your wardrobe categories, outfits and wear records; your try-on history, including outfits you try without saving and what started each try: a link, a post or the stylist, and the images in it; your wish list; your purchase reports and the sizes you bought; your style rules; your outfit discussions with the stylist; and the monthly and yearly cards you make, which never show sizes, measurements, fit assessments or body comparisons. A purchase you report can add the garment to your wardrobe in the size bought; undoing the report takes that addition back too, unless you have since changed its size, recorded how it fits, worn it, filed it in a category or put it in an ask.

When you capture a product page, in the extension or from the app, your own device reads the page; DressYou's server never fetches a retailer's page. The extension reads pages only while it is linked to your account, and records a purchase only when you review it from the toolbar. A garment scan crops a detected face on your device before the photograph is kept or sent. If a garment photograph shows a person, the server detects it only to keep the person out of the garment image, and keeps nothing from that detection except, when the photograph shows a whole person, the fact that it was refused.

To answer you, the stylist reads your wardrobe, outfits, wear records, try-on history and style rules, the shops your garments come from, and the shop, price and currency of a shop piece you discuss; never your recorded measurements or your pose answer. Of fit, it reads only whether a garment has a size answer for you and whether adding a measurement would give one. Comments on posts, hashtags and comments on asks never reach it. For now, OpenAI's text model reads your message, the conversation so far and the wardrobe facts the stylist needs, outside the European Economic Area until European processing is approved. Before a message you send is kept, TypeSafe looks in it for a figure such as a measurement, a size or a weight, hidden from the model when DressYou's checks find it, one they miss reaching the model; a statement that you are under 18 (section 9); distress; and sexual content, nudity, a judgement of a body, abuse, an attempt to change the stylist's rules or a request outside styling. A message asking for or describing sexual content involving a minor is refused and not kept; if it also says you are under 18, the pause of section 9 still applies. For the others, the stylist answers with a fixed message instead, one that points to help for distress, and keeps that answer, with your message, in the conversation; neither reaches the model later. TypeSafe also checks each reply before it is shown; a draft you have not sent is kept unchecked. Each message also carries your phone's time zone, to know which day you mean, which DressYou does not keep, and the app's language, kept to answer in it. If you connect your phone's calendar, which is optional, the app reads it on your phone, and a message about an occasion carries only that occasion's type, date, time and whether it lasts all day. DressYou does not store it. For the calendar, the legal basis is your consent, which you withdraw by disconnecting it.

Legal basis: the contract.

### 2.7 Posts and the feed

Your posts: your photographs, captions, hashtags and garment links, and your declaration that anyone else pictured agreed to appear. Your public name and, if you add one, your public photo, which is off by default. Your comments, likes, saves, follows and blocks. Likes and saves are shown without counts.

A photograph you post is read for its style, never for your body. In the first release the everyone tab orders posts with a small fixed rule from taste and engagement, and shows you lower a post whose garment comes in no size that fits you; commission never affects the order, and DressYou does not compare one person's body with another's. When another adult tries on a garment your post links, DressYou counts it toward your earned renders. Someone who shares your post outside DressYou shares only a link that opens it in DressYou; no copy of your photograph leaves DressYou that way.

Legal basis: the contract, since DressYou publishes what you ask it to. Who sees a post follows the poster's profile: a public profile's posts reach every signed-in account, and a followers profile's posts reach only the followers it accepted. A new profile starts at followers. A post's share link opens the App Store and shows nothing of the post outside DressYou.

### 2.8 Moderation and safety

Reports from accounts, with the reason chosen and any words added; decisions, with their statements of reasons; appeals; and account suspensions. Once posting opens, each post is screened before it is published: OpenAI's moderation and TypeSafe read the photograph, and TypeSafe the caption and hashtags. DressYou also takes a fingerprint (PDQ hash) of the photograph and compares it with your own recent renders, since a post must be a real photograph. DressYou keeps the record of each check with the post, and a post a check flags waits for a person's review. A photograph you delete while its post is being screened is kept until screening finishes, usually within minutes, then erased; while a check cannot answer, it is kept until one does. Arachnid Shield, which matches that fingerprint against known child sexual abuse material, is not enabled in the first release. Once it is, it receives only the fingerprint, before any other check, and if it matches a photograph to known child sexual abuse material, DressYou keeps the photograph, its post and a match record, which names the account's email and username so that a report to the authorities stays possible after the account is deleted, for 12 months from the match unless an authority's order extends that; the record is never mailed, logged or exported (section 6). TypeSafe also screens comments and profile text before another account reads them. Planned: notices from anyone, with the notifier's contact details if given.

DressYou uses this data to apply its rules and the law, to answer notices and to inform the authorities where the law requires. Legal basis: DressYou's legal obligations under the Digital Services Act (Article 6(1)(c)) and its legitimate interest in keeping the service safe.

While a report concerning a child's safety is under review, a post its author deletes is kept, withdrawn at once from every view, its author's included, and from the account's export: its photograph, caption, hashtags and garment links stay, only for the review. If the author deletes the account during the review, the account and its posts go, and only the photograph is kept, under a storage reference that identifies the account. Either is erased once the review is decided (section 6).

### 2.9 Ask a friend

When you ask friends for an opinion, DressYou keeps the outfit or the options you send, your optional question and, if you include them, copies of your garment photographs or an outfit image of your twin; by default friends see garments alone. Anyone with the link can open it and forward it.

A friend votes without an account. DressYou keeps the vote, an optional one-line comment and a random identifier set as a cookie on that link's pages; the database keeps only a hash of the identifier, and no IP address. Product pictures on the ask page load from the retailers' own servers, which therefore see the visit.

### 2.10 Purchases, renders and shop links

The paid tier and render purchases are paid to Apple or Google through their stores; DressYou never receives your card details. DressYou receives the store's record of each transaction and its notifications, and checks them on its own server to confirm what you bought. It also keeps your render balances: the free allowance, earned renders and purchased renders.

A shop link carries a new random reference on each tap, which never names your account. The retailer and its affiliate network record the visit under their own privacy notices, and the network later reports whether a purchase was approved, declined or amended. DressYou keeps a purchase record: the size bought, the dates, the size answer shown, your fit answer, and whether the garment was kept or returned, with the basis for it. You can delete a purchase record, and its influence leaves every value derived from it.

DressYou may test offers, prices and interfaces by assigning accounts to groups at random.

Legal basis: the contract; DressYou's legal obligations for accounting records.

### 2.11 Usage events, analytics and crash reports

DressYou records events in its own database. A few events record a step needed to deliver what you asked for, such as a capture you completed, the size you chose at purchase, a correction to your own record, or a garment you added or wore; they are recorded without asking. Every other event is recorded only with your consent, and each event keeps the consent state at the time it was recorded. Refusing costs no feature: you still save and use your own records. The app asks once, in one tap, the first time it would record such an event, and the usage analytics switch in the app's settings changes your choice at any time.

DressYou's server forwards only consented events, under your account's identifier and with only the properties a list allows, and aggregate figures to PostHog, hosted in the EU (Frankfurt). No analytics software runs in the apps, session replay is off, and no body data, measurement, pose answer or photograph reaches analytics. The aggregate figures include, for each day, the number of fit answers of each kind given on garments people own, counted over consented accounts and sent only when at least five people gave them; they name no one. When you delete your account, DressYou asks PostHog to delete the events it received under your identifier, which PostHog completes in its own cleanup; withdrawing consent stops new forwarding, and what PostHog already received stays there until then.

The apps send crash reports, and the server sends technical traces, to Sentry, hosted in the EU. They never carry an image, body data, a measurement or the pose answer.

### 2.12 Notifications, weather and support

The apps do not offer notifications yet. Once you allow them, DressYou keeps your device's push token, its platform, for Apple which of its two services issued it, and when it was first and last registered; the kinds of notification you turn on: votes on an ask and a support reply, and, once they are built, an ask's result, a friend's ask waiting for your vote, a render ready and the check-in after a purchase; and, for each notification owed to a device, its kind, the record of yours it concerns and whether it went. Apple's or Google's notification service delivers it carrying only its kind, that record's identifier, the key of the words your app shows and a default sound; your app supplies the words, so the service never receives them. Legal basis: your consent, which you withdraw by turning a kind off in the app, or notifications off in your device settings; the device's token stays until one of the events in section 6.

Weather is not in the app yet: today no location, weather or country code leaves your phone. Once it ships, it works as follows. The app asks to use your location the first time weather would change an outfit. If you allow it, the app reads your location at city level, only while you use it; it never tracks your location in the background and never uses your precise location. If you decline, outfit suggestions leave weather out. Each message you send the stylist carries the 10-day forecast and the 12 monthly averages for your phone's city, never your location. DressYou uses only the days you ask about and keeps none of it. The message also carries your city's country code, which DressYou does not keep. Apple's WeatherKit and Apple's map service receive your approximate location from your phone. On Android, Open-Meteo provides the forecast and receives your approximate location from your phone instead. Legal basis: your consent, which you withdraw in your device settings.

Support is one channel in the app. DressYou keeps your messages and its replies to answer you. Legal basis: the contract.

### 2.13 Being told when the app is out

If you ask on dressyouapp.com to be told when the app is out, DressYou keeps your email address to send you one message when the DressYou app is available, and for nothing else. Brevo (Sendinblue SAS, 9-17 rue Salneuve, 75017 Paris, France) stores the address for DressYou and first sends you an email to confirm it, so nobody can sign you up without you. DressYou deletes the address once the launch email is sent, or as soon as you unsubscribe. Legal basis: your consent, which you withdraw at any time through the link in every email, or by writing to the address in section 1.

## 3. What you must provide

An email address, or Sign in with Apple, is needed to create an account. Everything else is optional. Without body capture, try-ons are unavailable; without a garment you own with its size, or your measurements, DressYou gives no size answer and says so, and the try-on still works; without the pose answer, outfit images use a neutral pose.

## 7. Your rights

You have the right to access your data, to have it corrected or erased, to restrict its processing, to object to processing based on legitimate interest, and to receive the data you provided in a portable format. Where processing rests on your consent, you can withdraw it at any time, without affecting what was done before. Under French law you can also give instructions on what happens to your data after your death.

In the app you can export everything your account holds as one file, whose image links work for five minutes, other than a post held during a review concerning a child's safety and the records of your posts' screening (section 2.8), correct your records and measurements, and delete items or your whole account; export and deletion ask you to sign in again if your last sign-in is not recent. For anything else, write to the privacy contact in section 1. DressYou answers within one month; for a complex request the period can be extended by two more months, and DressYou tells you why within the first month.

You can complain to the [CNIL](https://www.cnil.fr/fr/plaintes) or to the supervisory authority of the EU country where you live or work.

## 8. Automated decisions

DressYou makes no decision about you with legal or similarly significant effects by automated means alone. Size answers are recommendations. Once posting opens, automatic screening holds a post a check flags, and a person then decides (section 2.8). Whether a refusal by the age check, or a hold by screening, is such a decision remains open (sections 2.2 and 2.8).

## 9. Age

DressYou is for people aged 18 or over. You confirm your age before capture, and an age check takes place before any photograph is processed (section 2.2). If you tell the stylist you are under 18, DressYou records a failed age check, which does not keep your words, pauses your twin, your try-ons, and the images and readings made from photographs of your own garments, and hides your account and posts from every other account; support can lift both when they were made in error. These records stay until you delete your account.

## 10. Security

Private files are stored encrypted, in storage no other account can reach, and the links that show them expire after five minutes. Two-step verification and passkeys protect sign-in.

## 11. Cookies and similar technologies

The apps contain no analytics software; crash reports are described in section 2.11. An ask page sets one cookie, limited to that ask's pages and lasting until the link ends, so that a friend's vote can be changed and counted once. The extension stores its link to your account in your browser. The website dressyouapp.com sets no cookie and runs no analytics or advertising.

## 12. Changes to this notice

When this notice changes in a way that matters, DressYou tells account holders in the app and by email before the change applies, and asks for acceptance again only when the change adds a new purpose.
